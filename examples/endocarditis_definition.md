**Endocarditis – Definition**

**Endocarditis** is an inflammatory condition of the **endocardium**, the endothelial lining of the heart, which predominantly affects the **cardiac valves**, but may also involve the mural endocardium, prosthetic valves and intracardiac devices.

**Infective endocarditis (IE)** is a potentially life-threatening infection of the endocardial surface, caused predominantly by bacteria and occasionally fungi, characterised by the formation of **infective vegetations** composed of platelets, fibrin, microorganisms and inflammatory cells.

These vegetations can cause:

* **Valvular destruction and regurgitation** due to infection-mediated tissue damage.
* **Systemic or pulmonary embolisation** due to fragmentation of vegetations.
* **Perivalvular extension** resulting in abscess formation and conduction abnormalities.
* **Immune-mediated complications**, including immune-complex glomerulonephritis.

**Key distinction:** Endocarditis may be infective or non-infective, although the term *endocarditis* in clinical practice most commonly refers to **infective endocarditis**.
