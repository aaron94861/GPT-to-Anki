# Acute Pancreatitis

## Definition
Acute inflammation of the pancreas, diagnosed with **2 of 3**: typical pain, lipase/amylase >3x ULN, imaging findings.

## Etiology
- Gallstones (most common)
- Alcohol
- Hypertriglyceridemia (>1000 mg/dL)
- Post-ERCP, drugs, hypercalcemia

## Pathogenesis
Premature activation of trypsinogen to trypsin within acinar cells leads to autodigestion and an inflammatory cascade.

## Physical Examination & Signs and Symptoms
- Severe epigastric pain radiating to the back
- Nausea and vomiting
- Epigastric tenderness, guarding
- *Grey Turner* sign (flank) and *Cullen* sign (periumbilical) in hemorrhagic cases

## Diagnosis
1. Serum lipase >3x ULN
2. Contrast CT if diagnosis unclear or no improvement in 48-72 h
3. RUQ ultrasound to look for gallstones

## Management & Treatment
- Aggressive IV fluids (lactated Ringer's)
- Pain control
- Early enteral feeding
- Cholecystectomy during the same admission for gallstone pancreatitis
